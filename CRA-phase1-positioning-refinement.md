# CRA-ENG Phase 1 Positioning Refinement

## Document Purpose

This document refines the Phase 1 website architecture before page copy is written or website files are changed.

It establishes a clear non-compete position:

> CRA-ENG is a China Execution Partner supporting companies that design, manufacture, convert, and upfit specialty mobile units.

CRA-ENG does not manufacture, convert, upfit, or sell finished vehicles. It manages agreed China-side component and supplier execution for the companies that do.

---

# 1. Positioning Foundation

## 1.1 Recommended Core Position

> CRA-ENG gives specialty vehicle and mobile equipment companies an accountable team in China to develop suppliers, coordinate custom components, validate first articles, control production, manage quality, consolidate supply, and support repeat orders.

## 1.2 Non-Compete Statement

Use a concise version on every market page:

> We support the companies that build specialized mobile units. CRA-ENG does not build, convert, upfit, or sell finished vehicles.

Use an expanded version where responsibility boundaries need to be explicit:

> The client owns the product, vehicle architecture, final integration, compliance decisions, and customer experience. CRA-ENG owns agreed China-side supplier and component execution.

## 1.3 Target Customers

Primary:

- Mobile grooming vehicle manufacturers
- Mobile grooming unit builders
- Grooming van conversion companies
- Specialty vehicle manufacturers
- Vehicle upfitters
- Mobile equipment manufacturers

Secondary:

- Mobile veterinary unit builders
- Mobile service unit manufacturers
- Equipment brands supplying specialized mobile units
- Outdoor and off-grid equipment companies

## 1.4 CRA-ENG Execution Scope

- Supplier development
- Custom component sourcing
- Engineering communication
- Prototype coordination
- First article validation
- Production readiness
- Quality control
- Supplier change control
- Multi-supplier consolidation
- Vehicle installation kits
- Replacement-part kits
- Repeat production supply

## 1.5 Positioning Exclusions

The website must not position CRA-ENG as:

- A mobile grooming vehicle manufacturer
- A vehicle conversion company
- A vehicle upfitter
- A finished equipment supplier
- A pet product company
- A general engineering consultant
- An electronics compliance company
- A generic sourcing agent
- A factory representative

The website must not claim:

- CRA-ENG owns factories, unless factually verifiable
- CRA-ENG manufactures complete vehicles
- CRA-ENG designs entire vehicle platforms
- CRA-ENG assumes final vehicle compliance or release responsibility
- CRA-ENG can manufacture everything

# 4. Homepage Positioning Recommendation

## 4.1 Headline Options Evaluated

### Option A

> Your China Execution Partner for Specialty Equipment Brands

### Strengths

- Broad enough for future market expansion
- Relevant to non-vehicle equipment brands
- Avoids implying that CRA-ENG builds vehicles
- Can accommodate outdoor, off-grid, and industrial products

### Weaknesses

- “Specialty equipment” is abstract
- It does not immediately signal the current vehicle and mobile-unit focus
- “Brands” may exclude manufacturers and vehicle upfitters that do not identify primarily as brands
- It creates a greater risk of attracting unrelated equipment inquiries

### Option B

> Your China Execution Partner for Specialty Vehicles and Mobile Equipment

### Strengths

- Immediately relevant to the primary target market
- Uses recognizable industry language
- Covers specialty vehicles, vehicle upfitters, and adjacent mobile equipment
- Supports mobile grooming, veterinary, service, outdoor, and off-grid applications
- Differentiates CRA-ENG from general consulting and generic sourcing

### Weaknesses

- Without supporting copy, a visitor could briefly wonder whether CRA-ENG works on complete vehicles
- It is narrower than “specialty equipment brands”

## 4.2 Recommended Long-Term Headline

> Your China Execution Partner for Specialty Vehicles and Mobile Equipment

## 4.3 Reason for Recommendation

Option B is more specific, memorable, and commercially useful. It aligns directly with the initial target customers and still allows adjacent expansion.

The potential ambiguity is solved through the supporting copy and a visible non-compete qualifier.

# 5. “Why Companies Need a China Execution Partner” Concept

## 5.1 Page and Homepage Section Purpose

Explain why direct factory management is often insufficient for specialized, low-to-medium volume component programs.

The goal is not to criticize Chinese factories. The goal is to explain the coordination gap between the client’s application requirements and each supplier’s production process.

## 5.2 Recommended Heading

> Why Companies Need a China Execution Partner

## 5.3 Recommended Introductory Direction

> A capable factory can still deliver the wrong result when application requirements, supplier decisions, prototypes, engineering changes, and production controls are not managed as one connected process. CRA-ENG closes that execution gap on the ground in China.

## 5.4 Customer Problems and CRA-ENG Responses

| Customer problem | CRA-ENG response |
|---|---|
| Communication gaps between overseas engineering teams and factories | Local technical communication, issue clarification, and decision tracking |
| Supplier misunderstanding of the installed application | Application review, critical requirement definition, and supplier-ready technical RFQs |
| Prototype differs from production | First article validation, golden samples, production readiness, and change control |
| Quality issues are discovered after shipment | In-process checks, pre-shipment verification, nonconformance reporting, and corrective actions |
| Several suppliers must deliver compatible parts | Interface coordination, common documentation, incoming verification, and multi-supplier consolidation |
| Engineering changes are communicated informally | Revision control, supplier acknowledgment, revalidation, and batch identification |
| Low-to-medium volume programs receive limited attention | Active production follow-up, escalation, and accountable project ownership |
| Individual components arrive but are not installation-ready | Matched sets, labels, installation hardware, packaging, and vehicle installation kits |

## 5.5 Recommended Page Structure

1. The execution gap
2. Why factory capability alone is not enough
3. Seven common failure points
4. CRA-ENG response to each failure point
5. Client, CRA-ENG, and supplier responsibilities
6. Example execution workflow
7. When to engage CRA-ENG
8. Project CTA

## 5.6 Responsibility Model

### Client owns

- Product strategy
- Vehicle and system architecture
- Customer requirements
- Compliance decisions
- Final integration and release
- Final commercial approvals

### CRA-ENG owns within the agreed project scope

- China-side technical communication
- Supplier development and qualification
- Prototype and first article coordination
- Production readiness
- Quality and change control
- Supplier follow-up
- Consolidation and kit preparation

### Suppliers own

- Manufacturing-process execution
- Process records and agreed controls
- Conformance to approved requirements
- Corrective actions for supplier-caused deviations

# 9. Anonymous Case Study Strategy

## 9.1 Recommended Working Title

> Vehicle Power Management Solution for a Specialty Mobile Unit

Alternative title if “solution” implies more design responsibility than CRA-ENG actually held:

> China-Side Supplier and Prototype Coordination for a Specialty Mobile Unit Power System

The alternative is safer when CRA-ENG’s primary role was execution rather than full system design.

## 9.2 Confidentiality Rules

Do not disclose:

- Customer name
- Customer logo
- Exact vehicle model
- Identifying product photography
- Proprietary electrical architecture
- Supplier name
- Confidential drawings
- Unique volumes, dates, or geographic details that identify the customer

Use only details approved for publication.

## 9.3 Recommended Case Study Structure

### Customer type

> Established specialty mobile unit manufacturer

Use a more specific category only if it does not identify the client.

### Industry

> Specialty vehicle / mobile service equipment

### Business context

Explain why the power-management component or assembly mattered to vehicle production, installation, field operation, or service.

### Challenge

Potential verified issues:

- Requirements needed to be translated for Chinese suppliers
- Several components or suppliers required coordination
- Prototype performance or interface needed evaluation
- Production readiness needed stronger controls
- Supplier communication and changes required local management

### CRA-ENG role

Include only verified responsibilities:

- Technical communication
- Supplier evaluation
- RFQ or specification clarification
- Prototype coordination
- First article inspection
- Issue tracking
- Supplier corrective action
- Production follow-up
- Quality verification
- Consolidation

### Supplier coordination

Show:

- How supplier questions were resolved
- How deviations were documented
- How interfaces between suppliers were managed
- How decisions were returned to the client

### Prototype evaluation

Show:

- What was inspected or tested
- What acceptance criteria were used
- What issues were found
- What required client approval

### Production support

Show:

- Production readiness activities
- Inspection or control approach
- Change-control measures
- Packaging or consolidation support

### Status or outcome

If results are verified:

- State only measurable, approved outcomes.

If results are not fully verified:

> The engagement demonstrates CRA-ENG’s execution approach across supplier communication, prototype coordination, first article review, and production preparation. Commercial or field-performance results are not disclosed.

Do not imply:

- Successful mass production
- Cost savings
- Improved reliability
- Certification success
- Reduced warranty claims
- Schedule improvement

unless each claim is verified and approved.

## 9.4 Required Case Study Images

- De-identified component or assembly
- Redacted technical review
- Prototype inspection
- Measurement or test setup
- Non-identifying supplier-production image
- Packaging or consolidation image

Use a schematic or simplified diagram when real photographs would reveal the client.
