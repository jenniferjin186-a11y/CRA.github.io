Why execution ownership matters

Supplier Development

Identify and assess suppliers against the actual process, material, quality, capacity, communication, volume, and commercial requirements of the program.

Typical activities: capability review, quotation alignment, risk identification, sample planning, quality-system review, and supplier follow-up.

Custom Component Development

Coordinate application-specific parts and fabricated assemblies against approved drawings, physical references, interfaces, and acceptance criteria.

Typical activities: requirement clarification, drawing and sample review, manufacturability feedback, deviation control, and supplier technical communication.

Prototype Validation

Manage prototype and first-article activity so decisions are documented before production release.

Typical activities: sample coordination, dimensional and functional evidence, issue logs, correction rounds, and approval-status control.

Production Readiness

Confirm that the approved component, manufacturing method, purchased parts, quality criteria, packaging, and production records are aligned before volume supply.

Typical activities: readiness reviews, control-point definition, golden-sample alignment, packaging confirmation, and release follow-up.

Quality Control

Establish practical evidence at the right stages instead of relying only on final inspection after the risks are already embedded.

Typical activities: incoming, in-process, pre-shipment, and corrective-action follow-up based on the agreed risk and scope.

Change Management

Keep revisions, substitutions, deviations, and process changes visible to the client and consistent across affected suppliers and production steps.

Typical activities: revision tracking, deviation records, approval routing, implementation checks, and replacement-part compatibility review.

Consolidation & Delivery

Coordinate components from multiple suppliers into complete, identified, and repeatable supply packages.

Typical activities: matched sets, vehicle installation kits, replacement packages, labeling, packing coordination, consolidation, and repeat-order control.
