# CRA-ENG Website Architecture

The site should position CRA-ENG as the China-side execution team for specialty-vehicle and mobile-equipment companies.

The architecture should move buyers through four questions:

1. Do you understand our industry?
2. Can you execute the components and systems we need?
3. Can we trust you to manage suppliers and quality in China?
4. What is the next step?

Core positioning:

> CRA-ENG is the China Execution Partner for specialty-vehicle and mobile-equipment companies—managing custom components, suppliers, prototypes, production, quality, and delivery.

# 2. Homepage Section Structure

## Section 1: Hero

The first screen must identify the audience, service, location, and outcome.

### Recommended headline

> Your China Execution Partner for Specialty Vehicles and Mobile Equipment

### Supporting copy

> We help specialty-vehicle manufacturers develop and produce custom components, assemblies, and equipment in China—from supplier qualification and prototypes through quality-controlled production and delivery.

### Primary CTA

> Discuss a Component or Supply Challenge

### Secondary CTA

> See How We Execute

### Immediate qualifier

> For mobile pet care vehicles, specialty conversions, mobile equipment, and outdoor/off-grid products.

The visual should show real vehicle systems, components, factory execution, inspections, or installed assemblies—not generic electronics or consulting imagery.

## Section 2: Buyer Problem

### Heading

> Managing Custom Components in China Should Not Require Managing Every Factory Yourself

Present four recognizable problems:

- Suppliers understand price but not the application.
- Approved samples change during production.
- Components arrive but fail during installation.
- Multiple suppliers create quality, schedule, and communication gaps.

Closing statement:

> CRA-ENG becomes your accountable China-side execution team.

## Section 3: What CRA-ENG Executes

### Heading

> From Technical Requirement to Production-Ready Supply

Use six concise capability cards:

1. Component and subsystem definition
2. Supplier search and qualification
3. Prototype and first-article coordination
4. Production and quality control
5. Multi-supplier consolidation and kitting
6. Delivery and repeat-order support

Each card should link to the relevant capability page.

## Section 4: Systems and Components

### Heading

> Components and Systems for Mobile Working Environments

Recommended categories:

- Water and wastewater systems
- Pumps, heaters, valves, and plumbing
- Mobile power and electrical distribution
- Batteries, inverters, chargers, and controls
- Tanks and molded components
- Stainless steel and aluminum assemblies
- Cabinets, interiors, and storage
- Mounting hardware and installation kits
- Ventilation and environmental-control components
- Service and replacement parts

This section makes the offer tangible.

## Section 5: Markets

### Heading

> Built Around the Needs of Specialty Product Companies

Four market cards:

- Mobile Pet Care Vehicles
- Specialty Vehicles
- Mobile Equipment
- Outdoor & Off-Grid Equipment

Every card should explain the relevant operating environment and link to a dedicated landing page.

## Section 6: Execution Model

### Heading

> One China-Based Team Across the Entire Supply Process

Recommended sequence:

1. Understand the application
2. Define requirements and acceptance criteria
3. Identify and qualify suppliers
4. Develop and validate samples
5. Control production
6. Inspect, consolidate, and deliver
7. Support changes and repeat orders

Emphasize ownership and documentation at every stage.

## Section 7: Why CRA-ENG

### Heading

> More Than Supplier Sourcing

Use direct comparisons:

| Typical sourcing agent | CRA-ENG |
|---|---|
| Finds available products | Develops against application requirements |
| Relies on supplier claims | Defines and verifies acceptance criteria |
| Focuses on purchase price | Manages technical, quality, and delivery risk |
| Introduces factories | Coordinates execution across suppliers |
| Ends at shipment | Supports repeat production and changes |

This distinction is central to the repositioning.

## Section 8: Evidence

### Heading

> Execution You Can Verify

Use only supportable evidence:

- Years of engineering/manufacturing experience
- Projects or suppliers managed
- Geographic manufacturing coverage
- Inspection and validation examples
- Real prototype or production photographs
- Anonymous client examples where NDAs apply

Avoid broad figures that cannot be substantiated.

## Section 9: Featured Case Study

### Structure

- Client type
- Component or subsystem
- Execution problem
- CRA-ENG’s responsibility
- Actions taken in China
- Validation performed
- Business result

CTA:

> View Execution Case Studies

## Section 10: Insights

Feature three high-intent articles:

- A market-specific problem
- A component/system engineering topic
- A supplier or quality-control topic

Avoid making a generic blog the central attraction.

## Section 11: Final CTA

### Heading

> Have a Component, Supplier, or Production Challenge in China?

### Copy

> Send us your drawings, specifications, existing sample, or current supplier problem. We will help determine the next practical execution step.

### CTA

> Start a Confidential Project Discussion

Supporting notes:

- NDA available
- Drawings and samples accepted
- Response within one business day, if operationally supportable

# 3. Page-by-Page Purpose

## Home

**Purpose:** Establish the new positioning and route qualified buyers to the correct market, capability, or inquiry page.

**Primary conversion:** Start a project discussion.

**Key message:** CRA-ENG manages China-side component and supply execution for specialty-vehicle companies.

## Markets Overview

**Purpose:** Demonstrate that CRA-ENG understands the working environments, supply problems, and operating requirements of its target customers.

**Primary conversion:** Select a relevant market or discuss an application.

This page should not be a generic collection of industries. Every market should share a logical connection: mobile, specialized, integrated equipment operating in demanding conditions.

## Mobile Pet Care Vehicles

**Purpose:** Serve as the primary vertical landing page for companies similar to Wag’n Tails.

**Audience:**

- Mobile grooming vehicle manufacturers
- Mobile veterinary vehicle builders
- Pet-care conversion companies
- Grooming-equipment brands
- Multi-location pet-care companies developing vehicle programs

**Page content:**

- Market-specific operational challenges
- Relevant systems and components
- CRA-ENG’s execution role
- Example supply programs
- Quality and validation approach
- Relevant insights
- Project CTA

**Important positioning:** CRA-ENG supports vehicle builders; it is not selling finished grooming vans to groomers.

## Specialty Vehicles

**Purpose:** Expand the offer beyond pet care without diluting the positioning.

**Audience:**

- Commercial van converters
- Mobile medical and veterinary units
- Utility and field-service vehicles
- Emergency and command vehicles
- Food-service and sanitation vehicles
- Mobile workshop manufacturers

**Page content:**

- Low-volume custom production challenges
- Multi-system component integration
- Fabricated parts and assemblies
- Change control
- Production continuity
- Replacement-part programs

## Mobile Equipment

**Purpose:** Attract equipment brands whose products are installed in vehicles, trailers, or transportable workspaces.

**Audience:**

- Mobile workshop brands
- Field-service equipment companies
- Vehicle-mounted equipment manufacturers
- Mobile sanitation and cleaning-equipment companies
- Portable commercial-equipment brands

**Page content:**

- Equipment integration
- Enclosures and mounting
- Power and control
- Preassembled kits
- Environmental validation
- Supplier coordination

## Outdoor & Off-Grid Equipment

**Purpose:** Reach adjacent companies with similar ruggedized power, water, fabrication, and supplier-management requirements.

**Audience:**

- Overland and expedition-equipment companies
- RV component brands
- Portable power brands

- Environmental durability

- Material selection
- Ruggedized assemblies
- Testing and certification
- OEM supplier management

**Recommended stages:**

1. Project intake
2. Technical definition
3. Supplier strategy
4. Quotation and qualification
5. Prototype development
6. Validation and approval
7. Production control
8. Inspection and delivery
9. Ongoing supply management

For every stage, explain:

- CRA-ENG’s responsibility
- Client input required
- Documentation produced
- Decision or approval gate

## Case Studies

**Purpose:** Provide evidence of execution capability.

**Case-study categories:**

- Component development
- Supplier recovery
- Water and plumbing systems
- Power and electrical components
- Fabricated assemblies
- Quality problems
- Multi-supplier kits
- Cost or lead-time improvement

Each case must prioritize operational evidence over marketing narrative.

## Insights

**Purpose:** Acquire qualified search traffic and demonstrate technical and operational understanding.

Recommended filters:

- Mobile Pet Care
- Specialty Vehicles
- Mobile Power
- Water Systems
- Supplier Management
- Quality Control

“Insights” is more appropriate than a generic “Blog.”

## About

**Purpose:** Answer why a foreign specialty-equipment company should trust CRA-ENG in China.

**Page content:**

- Company focus
- Engineering and manufacturing background
- China location and coverage
- Working model
- Independence from suppliers
- Communication approach
- Confidentiality
- Quality philosophy

The page should be specific and factual, not an abstract mission statement.

## NDA & Confidentiality

**Purpose:** Reduce hesitation around sharing drawings, samples, supplier problems, and unreleased products.

**Page content:**

- NDA availability
- Information handling
- Supplier disclosure controls
- Drawing and revision handling
- Photography policy
- Client ownership of project documentation
- Conflict handling

Only make commitments the business can operationally enforce.

## Start a Project

**Purpose:** Qualify inquiries and make the first conversation productive.

**Recommended form fields:**

- Company and website
- Product or vehicle category
- Component or system
- Current development stage
- Drawings available
- Physical sample available
- Current supplier status
- Expected annual volume
- Target market
- Required timing
- Main technical or supply problem
- NDA required
- File upload or secure file-exchange option

3. Custom Components & Assemblies
4. Fabricated Interiors & Hardware
5. Prototype Validation
6. Kitting, Consolidation & Delivery

These pages turn the positioning into tangible buying opportunities.

## Priority 3: Evidence

Develop as soon as credible material is available:

1. One mobile pet care or adjacent vehicle case study
2. One supplier-development case study
3. One prototype-to-production case study
4. One production-quality recovery case study
5. Real component and inspection photography
6. Example documentation with confidential details removed

Evidence will influence conversion more than publishing a large number of articles.

The first article cluster should focus on mobile grooming and specialty vehicles:

1. Components and systems inside a commercial mobile grooming vehicle
2. How to specify freshwater and wastewater tanks for mobile grooming vans
3. Common plumbing failures in mobile pet care vehicles
4. How to qualify a supplier for custom specialty-vehicle components
5. First article inspection for fabricated vehicle components
6. Preventing supplier changes after sample approval
7. When to source individual components versus complete installation kits
8. How multi-supplier kitting reduces specialty-vehicle assembly time
9. Designing imported components for installation and field service
10. Building a replacement-parts program for specialty vehicles

## Priority 5: Adjacent-Market Expansion

After the core specialty-vehicle positioning gains traction:

1. Mobile Equipment market page
2. Outdoor & Off-Grid market page
3. Power-system content cluster
4. Outdoor durability content cluster
5. Vehicle-mounted equipment content
6. RV and overland component content

This sequence prevents the initial message from becoming too broad.

# Recommended Minimum Viable Launch

A credible first version does not need every proposed page. The minimum structure should be:

```text
Home
Markets
├── Mobile Pet Care Vehicles
└── Specialty Vehicles
Capabilities
├── Custom Components & Assemblies
├── Water & Power Systems
├── Supplier Development
├── Production & Quality Control
└── Kitting & Delivery
How We Execute
Case Studies
About
Start a Project
```

Launch depth matters more than page count. Ten specific, evidence-based pages will outperform thirty generic service pages.

The entire architecture should reinforce one commercial idea:

> CRA-ENG gives specialty-vehicle companies an accountable execution team on the ground in China—from component requirement to reliable production supply.
