## Section 3: Where Direct Factory Management Breaks Down

### Heading

Seven Common Execution Gaps

### 1. Communication Gaps

Supplier questions move between engineering, procurement, translation, and sales contacts. Technical context is lost, assumptions replace decisions, and open issues are not consistently tracked.

**CRA-ENG response:** Coordinate China-side technical communication, clarify questions, document decisions, and escalate unresolved deviations.

### 2. Supplier Misunderstanding

A supplier may quote the drawing without understanding the installation, interface, duty cycle, service requirement, or consequence of a material change.

**CRA-ENG response:** Review the application context, identify critical requirements, and align suppliers before prototypes and production.

### 3. Prototype and Production Differences

The supplier gives special attention to a sample, then uses different material, tooling, process parameters, purchased parts, or workmanship during repeat production.

**CRA-ENG response:** Coordinate first article validation, approved reference samples, production readiness, inspection requirements, and supplier change control.

### 4. Quality Problems Discovered Too Late

A pre-shipment visual check cannot recover weeks of production or confirm every critical material, interface, or functional requirement.

**CRA-ENG response:** Build quality activities into supplier selection, prototype review, first article approval, production readiness, in-process follow-up, and final release.

### 5. Multiple Suppliers Work in Isolation

Each supplier produces its own part, but no supplier owns the compatibility of the complete installation.

**CRA-ENG response:** Coordinate shared interfaces, revisions, incoming verification, matched component sets, and multi-supplier consolidation.

### 6. Engineering Changes Are Not Controlled

A revised drawing is emailed, but old files remain in circulation. A purchased part changes. A material becomes unavailable. A supplier implements a change without formal reapproval.

**CRA-ENG response:** Track approved revisions, require supplier acknowledgment, document proposed deviations, coordinate revalidation, and identify affected production.

### 7. Low-to-Medium Volume Programs Lose Attention

Specialty vehicle programs may not command the volume of a major automotive contract. Schedule, quality, and response discipline can decline after the first order.

**CRA-ENG response:** Maintain active supplier follow-up, track open issues, escalate delays and deviations, and support controlled repeat supply.
