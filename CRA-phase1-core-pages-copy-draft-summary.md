# 1. Key Messaging Decisions

## 1.1 Lead With China Execution

### Decision

The homepage leads with:

> Your China Execution Partner for Specialty Vehicles and Mobile Equipment

### Reason

This immediately identifies CRA-ENG’s geography, role, and target market. It avoids the ambiguity of general engineering consulting and the price-led positioning of generic sourcing.

---

## 1.2 Build the Non-Compete Boundary Into Every Core Page

### Decision

The pages state:

> We support the companies that build specialized mobile units. CRA-ENG does not build, convert, upfit, or sell finished vehicles.

The mobile grooming page uses a more specific equivalent.

### Reason

The target audience includes vehicle manufacturers, conversion companies, and vehicle upfitters. CRA-ENG must be understood as an extension of their China-side capability, not a competitor for their finished vehicle customers.

---

## 1.3 Define CRA-ENG Through Operational Responsibilities

### Decision

The copy consistently explains CRA-ENG through:

- Supplier development
- Custom component sourcing
- Engineering communication
- Prototype coordination
- First article validation
- Production readiness
- Quality control
- Supplier change control
- Multi-supplier consolidation
- Vehicle installation kits
- Repeat production supply

### Reason

Specific execution responsibilities are more credible and commercially useful than generic statements about consulting, project support, or “one-stop” service.

---

## 1.4 Explain Why Factory Management Alone Is Insufficient

### Decision

The “Why Companies Need a China Execution Partner” page focuses on seven execution gaps:

1. Communication gaps
2. Supplier misunderstanding
3. Prototype-to-production differences
4. Quality discovered too late
5. Suppliers operating in isolation
6. Weak engineering change control
7. Low-to-medium volume programs losing attention

### Reason

Prospective customers need to understand why they should use CRA-ENG instead of dealing directly with factories or hiring only a final inspection provider.

---

## 1.5 Avoid Criticizing Factories

### Decision

The copy explains that a factory owns its manufacturing process while the client remains responsible for the complete product.

### Reason

The commercial problem is not that factories are inherently incapable. The problem is the execution gap between application requirements, several suppliers, prototypes, production, changes, and delivery.

This framing is more accurate and professional.

---

## 1.6 Use a Three-Party Responsibility Model

### Decision

The copy distinguishes:

- What the client owns
- What CRA-ENG owns within the agreed scope
- What suppliers own

### Reason

Clear responsibility prevents CRA-ENG from appearing to own vehicle design, final integration, compliance, or product release. It also demonstrates a controlled B2B operating model.

---

## 1.7 Position Mobile Grooming as an Industrial Application

### Decision

The mobile grooming page discusses:

- Water and wastewater components
- Pumps and plumbing assemblies
- Auxiliary power and electrical components
- Fabricated interiors
- Cabinets and mounting hardware
- Vehicle installation kits
- Replacement parts
- Repeat production supply

### Reason

The target is the company building the specialized mobile unit—not groomers, pet owners, or consumers. Component, production, installation, and service language reinforces the B2B position.

---

## 1.8 Make Installation Kits a Differentiator

### Decision

Vehicle installation kits receive a dedicated section.

### Reason

Manufacturers frequently receive loose components from several suppliers and must resolve missing parts, configuration differences, and identification problems during assembly.

Matched, labeled, revision-controlled kits turn separate supplier outputs into something directly useful to the client’s production team.

---

## 1.9 Treat Repeat Supply and Replacement Parts as Part of Execution

### Decision

The mobile grooming page extends the message beyond the first order to:

- Revision-controlled repeat production
- Approved changes
- Part identification
- Model-specific replacement kits
- Service supply

### Reason

Specialty vehicles remain in service for years. Component continuity and configuration control are important to production, warranty, and after-sales teams.

---

## 1.10 Keep the Anonymous Case Study Conservative

### Decision

The case-study preview focuses on:

- Supplier communication
- Prototype coordination
- Evaluation
- Issue follow-up
- Production preparation

It does not claim cost savings, production success, improved reliability, certification results, or other unverified outcomes.

### Reason

The copy should demonstrate relevance without overstating CRA-ENG’s design ownership or project results.

---

## 1.11 Use Practical CTAs

### Decision

Primary calls to action include:

- Discuss a Component or Supply Challenge
- Discuss Your China Execution Gap
- Discuss a Grooming Vehicle Component
- Start a Confidential Project Discussion

### Reason

These CTAs match how B2B manufacturing projects begin. They invite the visitor to present an existing technical or supplier problem rather than requesting a generic consultation or consumer quote.
