# 1. Key Decisions and Reasons

## Decision 1: Establish an Explicit Non-Compete Position

### Decision

CRA-ENG will be positioned as a China Execution Partner that supports companies building specialized mobile units.

Required boundary:

> We support the companies that build specialized mobile units. CRA-ENG does not build, convert, upfit, or sell finished vehicles.

### Reason

The target customers are vehicle manufacturers, conversion companies, and vehicle upfitters. If CRA-ENG appears to manufacture vehicles, it could be perceived as a competitor rather than a partner.

The non-compete boundary reduces that risk and makes CRA-ENG’s role easier to understand.

---

## Decision 2: Use “Specialty Vehicles and Mobile Equipment” in the Homepage Headline

### Decision

Recommended headline:

> Your China Execution Partner for Specialty Vehicles and Mobile Equipment

### Reason

This is more specific and commercially relevant than:

> Your China Execution Partner for Specialty Equipment Brands

“Specialty vehicles and mobile equipment” directly reflects the initial target market. “Specialty equipment brands” is broader but more abstract, can exclude manufacturers that do not identify as brands, and may attract unrelated inquiries.

The supporting copy and non-compete qualifier resolve any risk that the headline implies complete vehicle manufacturing.

---

## Decision 3: Rename Market Pages Around Support

### Decision

Use:

1. Mobile Grooming Vehicle Components & Supply Support
2. Specialty Vehicle Components & Supply Chain Support
3. Mobile Equipment Components & Manufacturing Support
4. Outdoor & Off-Grid Equipment Components & Supply Support

### Reason

Earlier working names such as “Specialty Vehicle Manufacturers” can imply that CRA-ENG is the manufacturer. The revised names identify the market while clearly framing CRA-ENG’s role around components, supply support, and manufacturing execution.

---

## Decision 4: Make Mobile Grooming Vehicle Support the Primary Market Entry

### Decision

The first and most developed market page will target:

- Mobile grooming vehicle manufacturers
- Mobile grooming unit builders
- Grooming van conversion companies
- Related vehicle upfitters

### Reason

This is the closest fit to companies similar to Wag’n Tails. It offers a concrete component scope:

- Water and wastewater systems
- Pumps and plumbing assemblies
- Auxiliary power
- Electrical components
- Fabricated interiors
- Cabinets
- Mounting hardware
- Installation kits
- Replacement parts

The page will remain strictly B2B and will state that CRA-ENG does not sell finished grooming vehicles.

---

## Decision 5: Define CRA-ENG’s Execution Responsibility

### Decision

CRA-ENG’s agreed scope may include:

- Supplier development
- Custom component sourcing
- Engineering communication
- Prototype coordination
- First article validation
- Production readiness
- Quality control
- Supplier change control
- Multi-supplier consolidation
- Vehicle installation kits
- Repeat production supply

### Reason

This scope is specific enough to distinguish CRA-ENG from general consultants and generic sourcing agents. It also reflects the operational problems specialty vehicle companies face when managing several suppliers and custom, low-to-medium volume components.

---

## Decision 6: Adopt a Clear Responsibility Model

### Decision

Use:

> The client owns the product and customer experience. CRA-ENG owns agreed China-side execution.

More specifically:

### Client owns

- Product strategy
- Vehicle and system architecture
- Customer requirements
- Compliance decisions
- Final integration and release
- Final approvals

### CRA-ENG owns within the agreed scope

- China-side technical communication
- Supplier development and qualification
- Prototype and first article coordination
- Production readiness
- Quality and change control
- Supplier follow-up
- Consolidation and kit preparation

### Reason

This prevents scope confusion and protects the target customer’s ownership of its vehicle, product, brand, compliance decisions, and customer relationship.

---

## Decision 7: Add “Why Companies Need a China Execution Partner”

### Decision

Create a dedicated page and a condensed homepage section explaining:

- Communication gaps
- Supplier misunderstanding
- Prototype-to-production differences
- Late quality discovery
- Multi-supplier coordination
- Engineering change management
- Low-to-medium volume neglect

Then connect each problem to:

- Technical communication
- Supplier qualification
- Prototype control
- Production follow-up
- Quality management
- Change control
- Consolidation

### Reason

The site must explain why a capable buyer should hire CRA-ENG instead of directly managing factories. Without this section, visitors may understand the services but not the commercial need for an execution partner.

---

## Decision 8: Refine the About Position

### Decision

The About page will emphasize that CRA-ENG is:

- Engineering-led
- China-based
- Supplier-independent
- Execution-focused
- Experienced in OEM manufacturing and supplier management

It will not present CRA-ENG as a large factory or vehicle manufacturer.

### Reason

Qualified B2B buyers need to understand who represents their interests and whether supplier recommendations are independent. Factual, restrained positioning creates more trust than trying to appear like a large vertically integrated manufacturer.

---

## Decision 9: Use an Anonymous Case Study Carefully

### Decision

Recommended working title:

> China-Side Supplier and Prototype Coordination for a Specialty Mobile Unit Power System

Potential marketing title:

> Vehicle Power Management Solution for a Specialty Mobile Unit

Use the safer execution-oriented title if CRA-ENG did not own the complete system design.

The case study will cover:

- Customer type
- Industry
- Challenge
- CRA-ENG role
- Supplier coordination
- Prototype evaluation
- Production support

No customer name or unverified result will be published.

### Reason

An anonymous case can demonstrate relevance while preserving confidentiality. Execution-oriented wording prevents the site from overstating CRA-ENG’s engineering ownership or project outcomes.

---

## Decision 10: Build SEO Around B2B Component and Execution Intent

### Decision

Combine market terms with:

- Component
- Supplier
- Manufacturer
- Builder
- Upfitter
- OEM
- Production
- Quality
- Supply chain
- Installation kit

### Reason

Market terms alone can attract consumer vehicle buyers or make CRA-ENG appear to sell complete vehicles. B2B qualifiers attract companies seeking component and supplier support.

Consumer searches such as grooming vans for sale, grooming van prices, grooming services, and pet supplies will not be targeted.

# 2. Changes Compared With `CRA-phase1-content-plan.md`

## 2.1 Page Names Changed

| Earlier name | Refined name |
|---|---|
| Mobile Grooming & Pet Service Vehicles | Mobile Grooming Vehicle Components & Supply Support |
| Specialty Vehicle Manufacturers | Specialty Vehicle Components & Supply Chain Support |
| Mobile Equipment | Mobile Equipment Components & Manufacturing Support |

### Why

The earlier names could make CRA-ENG appear to be the manufacturer. The refined names explicitly position CRA-ENG as a component and supply-chain support partner.

---

## 2.2 Non-Compete Language Elevated

### Earlier plan

The content plan included audience qualifiers and stated that CRA-ENG does not sell finished grooming vans.

### Refined plan

The non-compete position is now a sitewide rule:

> We support the companies that build specialized mobile units. We do not build, convert, upfit, or sell finished vehicles.

### Why

This issue affects the entire brand position, not only the mobile grooming page.

---

## 2.3 Homepage Recommendation Narrowed

### Earlier plan

The homepage used “Your China Execution Partner for Specialty Vehicles and Mobile Equipment” without a formal comparison against the broader alternative.

### Refined plan

Both headline options were evaluated, and “Specialty Vehicles and Mobile Equipment” was selected as the long-term recommendation.

### Why

The selected phrase provides better initial-market relevance while remaining broad enough for mobile equipment and off-grid expansion.

---

## 2.4 New Dedicated Education Page Added

### Added

`why-china-execution-partner.html`

### Why

The earlier plan explained buyer problems within the homepage and individual pages. The refinement makes the commercial need for CRA-ENG a distinct, reusable argument.

---

## 2.5 Responsibility Model Made More Explicit

### Earlier plan

The content plan described client and CRA-ENG responsibilities primarily on Capabilities, How We Execute, and About.

### Refined plan

The responsibility model is now a core positioning framework used throughout the site.

### Why

It prevents CRA-ENG from appearing to own the complete vehicle, system architecture, compliance decision, or customer relationship.

---

## 2.6 Case Study Title Made Safer

### Earlier plan

The plan recommended representative engagements and factual case studies but did not formalize this specific anonymous example.

### Refined plan

The case study strategy now distinguishes between:

- A broader “solution” title
- A safer supplier-and-prototype-coordination title

### Why

The chosen title must accurately reflect whether CRA-ENG owned system design or only China-side execution.

---

## 2.7 SEO Intent Tightened

### Earlier plan

The plan targeted mobile grooming vehicle and specialty vehicle terms with B2B qualifiers.

### Refined plan

Every market cluster now follows:

> Market/application + component/supplier/execution intent

### Why

This further reduces consumer traffic and the risk that search snippets portray CRA-ENG as a finished vehicle supplier.

---

## 2.8 Adjacent Markets Reduced in Initial Priority

### Earlier plan

All four market groups were included in the Phase 1 page plan.

### Refined plan

Mobile equipment and outdoor/off-grid pages remain in the architecture but can launch after the two priority vehicle-support pages if evidence or content depth is limited.

### Why

The initial website should establish a sharp position before expanding.
